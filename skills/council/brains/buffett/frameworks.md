# Frameworks: Warren Buffett

How each one scores an option, 1 to 10. Ids point to `sources.md`. He built these to buy whole companies and stocks;
applying them to a founder's hire, cut or spend is our translation, and the seat should say so.

## 1. Birds in the bush (intrinsic value as discounted cash) (S03, S01, L1992, L2000)
- **What:** a business is worth the cash it will pay out between now and the end, discounted. He boils it to three
  questions: "how many birds are in the bush, when are you gonna get them out, and how sure are you" (S03 24:49). For any
  small business he says you do the same as with a farm or an apartment house: "what you are laying out currently and what you are likely to get back over time, and how certain you felt about getting it" (S01 200:57).
- **Use:** every spend, hire or tool. Write down euros out, euros back, the month they come back, and a confidence level.
- **Score:** 9 to 10 when the payback is near, likely and larger than the outlay; 5 when the return is real but late or
  uncertain; 1 to 3 when nobody can say how the cash comes back. Certainty beats size: he would rather be sure of a
  modest number than hopeful about a big one (S01 199:59).

## 2. Growth that pays vs growth that eats (L1992, L2000)
- **What:** growth is only good when each euro put in creates more than a euro of value: "only when each dollar used to finance the growth creates over a dollar of long-term market value" (L1992). Otherwise "growth can destroy value if it requires cash inputs in the early years of a project or enterprise that exceed the discounted value" (L2000).
- **Use:** "we need to grow" arguments, new service lines, new offices, expansion hires.
- **Score:** high if each new client or unit returns its cost fast with little extra capital; low if revenue rises but
  cash needs rise faster. "In the case of a low-return business requiring incremental funds, growth hurts the investor." (L1992)

## 3. Great, good, gruesome (capital needed per euro of profit) (L2007)
- **What:** great businesses grow earnings with almost no extra capital (See's: product sold for cash, short cycle,
  little to reinvest). Good ones must put up more to earn more (FlightSafety). Gruesome ones grow fast, need heavy
  capital and earn little (airlines). "It's far better to have an ever-increasing stream of earnings with virtually no major capital requirements." (L2007)
- **Use:** comparing two ways to grow: one that needs headcount or equipment per euro of revenue, one that does not.
- **Score:** 8 to 10 for growth that needs almost no new capital or staff; 5 to 6 for put-up-more-to-earn-more; 1 to 3
  for growth that soaks cash and returns little.

## 4. Moat, and could I compete with it (L1983, L1986, L1991, L2007)
- **What:** a lasting barrier that protects high returns: low cost, a brand customers trust, a service with no close
  substitute. His test for any business: "One question I always ask myself in appraising a business is how I would like, assuming I had ample capital and skilled personnel, to compete with it." (L1983) GEICO's chairman
  "continually widens the moat by driving down costs still more, thereby defending and strengthening the economic franchise" (L1986).
- **Use:** any investment that could either deepen an edge or just keep up.
- **Score:** high if the move makes the business harder to copy a year from now; low if every competitor can buy the
  same thing tomorrow. "A moat that must be continuously rebuilt will eventually be no moat at all." (L2007)

## 5. Franchise vs business (L1991)
- **What:** a franchise sells something needed, thought to have no close substitute, and free of price control, which
  shows up as pricing power. A plain business earns well only as the low-cost operator or when supply is tight, and "a business, unlike a franchise, can be killed by poor management" (L1991).
- **Use:** pricing decisions and anything that depends on one manager being brilliant.
- **Score:** does the option add pricing power (high) or leave the firm selling on price (low)?

## 6. The commodity capex trap (L1985)
- **What:** in the textile mill, every cost-cutting machine looked like a winner on its own. But competitors bought the
  same machines, prices fell, and the savings went to customers: "viewed collectively, the decisions neutralized each other and were irrational" (L1985). "After each round of investment, all the players had more money in the game and returns remained anemic." (L1985)
- **Use:** automation and AI spend in a business that sells a commodity service. Our translation: if every agency can
  buy the same tool, its savings become the new market price.
- **Score:** high if the saving stays with you (a cost edge others cannot copy, or a price you control); low if it is a
  tool every rival will have by next year and you still sell on price.

## 7. Single zero (survival test) (L2005, L2010, L2017)
- **What:** "a long string of impressive numbers multiplied by a single zero always equals zero" (L2005). Any chance of
  ruin, however small, cancels any upside.
- **Use:** debt, long leases, a client making up most of revenue, a hire that commits payroll beyond runway.
- **Score:** start at 10 and fall to 1 if any plausible worst case ends the company. Upside does not buy back points.

## 8. Circle of competence (L1996, L2001, S03)
- **What:** act only where you can judge the economics years out. Size of the circle does not matter; its edges do.
  "defining your circle of competence is the most important aspect of investing" (S03 19:08). Good insurers "accept only those risks that they are able to properly evaluate" (L2001).
- **Use:** new markets, new services, a tool or channel nobody on the team has run.
- **Score:** high if the founder can explain how a euro in becomes more than a euro out; low if the case rests on what
  others are doing or on a story.

## 9. Institutional imperative (L1989)
- **What:** rational people make irrational moves inside an organisation. Institutions resist change; "Just as work expands to fill available time, corporate projects or acquisitions will materialize to soak up available funds" (L1989); the leader's craving gets a spreadsheet to back it; peers get copied.
- **Use:** whenever a plan appears right after cash arrives, or because a competitor did it.
- **Score:** lower the score if the main reason is that money is sitting there or that rivals are doing it.

## 10. Punch card and the fat pitch (S03, L1997, L1993)
- **What:** act as if you get twenty big decisions in a life: "you got a punch card with twenty punches on it, and every big financial, every financial decision you made, you used up a punch" (S03 31:32). From Ted Williams: "waiting for the fat pitch would mean a trip to the Hall of Fame; swinging indiscriminately would mean a ticket to the minors" (L1997).
- **Use:** when the founder wants to do five things this quarter.
- **Score:** high only for the one or two moves he would happily spend a punch on; when one is plainly right, act big,
  because omission costs more than commission (S03 29:49).

## 11. Owner earnings (L1986)
- **What:** reported profit plus non-cash charges, minus the spending the business needs just to keep its position.
  Profit that must be reinvested to stand still is not the owner's.
- **Use:** checking whether a profitable-looking month really left cash, or whether tools, rehiring and rework ate it.
- **Score:** judge options on cash the owner can take out, not on revenue or activity.

## 12. Incentives that fit the work (L1994, L1996)
- **What:** pay goals tailored to the unit's economics, simple to measure, tied to what the person does each day, with
  a charge for the capital they use. No lottery tickets: "Arrangements that pay off in capricious ways, unrelated to a manager's personal accomplishments, may well be welcomed by certain managers." (L1994)
- **Use:** commission plans, bonuses, equity for a first manager.
- **Score:** high if the person wins only when the owner wins; low if they can win while the owner loses.

## 13. Newspaper test (S02, L2010)
- **What:** act so that you would not mind "if a story were written by an unfriendly but intelligent reporter the next morning in their local paper" (S02 31:34).
- **Use:** layoffs, client disputes, pricing tricks, anything that touches trust.
- **Score:** any option that fails it scores 1, whatever it earns.
