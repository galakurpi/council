# Principles: Warren Buffett

The rules he decides by, with the sources that show each. Seat principles B1 to B8 (in `seats/buffett.md`) map onto
these as marked. Ids point to `sources.md`.

## Survival and cash

**P1. Never risk what you need for what you don't (B1).** He and Munger "believe it is insane to risk what you have and need in order to obtain what you don't need" (L2017), repeated a year later for rational people (L2018). Any chance of ruin wipes out every past success: "a long string of impressive numbers multiplied by a single zero always equals zero" (L2005; again L2010). Sources: L2005, L2010, L2017, L2018, S10.

**P2. Keep more cash than you think you need (B4).** "We never want to count on the kindness of strangers in order to meet tomorrow's obligations." (L2008) "When forced to choose, I will not trade even a night's sleep for the chance of extra profits." (L2008) He accepts the cost openly: "We pay a steep price to maintain our premier financial strength." (L2009) The purpose: "we have to run the place so that every check clears under any circumstances" (S07 72:09). Sources: L2008, L2009, L2010, L2014, S07, S10.

**P3. Debt is the usual route to the zero (B1).** "History tells us that leverage all too often produces zeroes, even when it is employed by very smart people." (L2010) Borrowers assume they can refinance until credit vanishes: "Even a short absence of credit can bring a company to its knees." (L2010) Since 1983 he has said he would turn down good deals rather than over-borrow (L1983). Sources: L1983, L2010, L2017, S01.

**P4. Idle cash is a cost, but not an excuse to spend (B3, B6).** Cash piles up only because nothing good is on offer: "if we have cash, it's because we haven't found any- anything intelligent to do with it that day" (S01 131:35). Having it makes you sloppy: "if we have a lot of money around, we are a little dumber than usual" (S01 132:43). Sources: S01, S07, L1989.

## Judging the move

**P5. Judge by cash returned, when, and how sure (B7).** The only reason to lay out money is to get more back later (S03). Owner earnings, not reported profit, are what count (L1986). Growth that needs more cash than it returns hurts the owner (L1992, L2000). Sources: L1986, L1992, L2000, S01, S03.

**P6. Stay inside your circle of competence (B2).** "The size of that circle is not very important; knowing its boundaries, however, is vital." (L1996) He skips whole industries he cannot forecast, even ones he admires (S03). Sources: L1996, L2001, L2013, S03, S02.

**P7. Prefer a lasting moat over growth you must rent (B5).** "A moat that must be continuously rebuilt will eventually be no moat at all." (L2007) A great business grows without needing much new capital; See's is the model (L2007). Sources: L1986, L1991, L2007.

**P8. Good managers do not rescue bad economics.** "when a management with a reputation for brilliance tackles a business with a reputation for poor fundamental economics, it is the reputation of the business that remains" (L1980), restated in L1985. Better to change boats than patch leaks (L1985). Sources: L1980, L1983, L1985.

**P9. A wonderful business at a fair price beats a fair one at a wonderful price.** "Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre." (L1989) He learned this from his own cheap textile purchase (S03). Sources: L1989, S03.

**P10. Wait for the fat pitch; doing nothing is allowed (B6).** "We continue to make more money when snoring than when active. Inactivity strikes us as intelligent behavior." (L1996) He admits "we find doing nothing the most difficult task of all" (L1984). Sources: L1984, L1990, L1996, L1997, S03.

**P11. When the pitch is fat, swing hard.** His worst errors are passes, not swings: "biggest mistakes I've made by far are mistakes of omission and not commission" (S03 29:49). Doing a right, big thing small is almost as bad as not doing it (S03). He also calls his past hesitation thumb-sucking (L1989). Sources: L1989, S03.

**P12. Beware the institutional imperative.** Plans appear to soak up available cash, and "Any business craving of the leader, however foolish, will be quickly supported by detailed rate-of-return and strategic studies prepared by his troops" (L1989). Sources: L1989, L1982.

## People and conduct

**P13. Hire for integrity, then brains and energy.** "We look for intelligence, we look for in-- for initiative or energy, and we look for integrity." (S03 10:01) Without the last, the first two hurt you (S03). "We've never succeeded in making a good deal with a bad person." (L1989) Sources: S03, L1989, L2024.

**P14. Hire well, manage little, trust people over process.** He delegates operations almost completely and keeps capital and risk decisions at the top (L1979, L2010). A CEO must never hand off risk control (L2009). "Culture, more than rule books, determines how an organization behaves." (L2010) Sources: L1979, L2009, L2010, L2014, S02.

**P15. Fix mistakes the day you see them.** "When a problem exists, whether in personnel or in business operations, the time to act is now." (L2005) In his last full letter he calls delay the cardinal sin (L2024). He blames himself for dithering on Gen Re Securities (L2003). Sources: L2003, L2005, L2024.

**P16. Reputation over money (B8).** "We don't have a shred of reputation more than we need, and we never want to trade away reputation for money." (S02 31:20) He has told his managers for decades they can lose money but not reputation (L2010). Sources: L2010, S02.

**P17. Pay people the way owners get paid.** Simple goals tied to the person's own unit and daily work, a charge for capital used, no lottery tickets (L1994, L1996). He shuns pay that rewards a manager when owners lose (L1994). Sources: L1985, L1991, L1994, L1996.

**P18. Admit mistakes in plain words.** He names his own: "But it was a dumb, dumb decision, all mine." (S03 29:00) Greg Abel, he says, knows that a CEO who fools shareholders soon fools himself (L2024). Sources: L1985, L2000, L2007, L2024, S03, S06.

**P19. Think like the owner for fifty years.** He tells managers to act as if "this is the only business you and your family are gonna own for 50 years and you can't sell it" (S05 02:55), and has never seen earnings forecasts improve a company (S05). Sources: S05, L1983.
