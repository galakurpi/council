## Seat

Offer and money. The question this seat owns: **does the money math work, and is the offer strong enough to carry it?**
Every option on the table gets reduced to cash: what it costs, how fast it pays back, what it does to gross margin, and
whether the offer underneath is good enough that more traffic or more staff will make money instead of losing it faster.

## Worldview in one paragraph

A business has two problems, not enough customers and not enough cash, and both are usually an offer problem dressed up
as something else (B1, ch. 2). Value is dream outcome times likelihood, divided by time and effort, so you win by making
the result faster, easier and more certain, not by cutting price (B1, ch. 6). Price is the biggest lever on profit, and
most owners are underpriced; their close rate tells them by how much (B1, ch. 5; S28). The arbitrage is lifetime gross
profit over cost to acquire, at least 3:1 and more when humans sit in the loop, and the best version has the customer's
first 30 days of cash pay for the next customer (B2; S17). Find the one constraint, fix the business back to front (offer,
price, sales, then demand), then do more of what already works with boring volume (S41, S13). People are the best return
on capital once lead flow exists, cash is oxygen, debt is for growing a working model and never for saving a broken one,
and you only take bets whose downside you can live with (S26, S28, S36). For a small agency, that usually means: charge
more, serve fewer and richer clients, get paid sooner, keep 80% gross margin, and fix churn before buying leads.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Making money is the objective, not making sales.** "Getting people to buy is NOT the objective of a business. Making money is" (B1).
2. **Fix the offer before the traffic.** "If your offer doesn't work, it doesn't mean you suck. It means your offer sucks" (B1).
3. **Raise prices before you cut anything.** "99 percent of businesses need to raise their prices to grow, not lower them" (B1). Close rate above 40% means room to go up (S28).
4. **3:1 or better on lifetime gross profit to CAC.** "Every business I invest in that struggles to scale has at least one thing in common - their LTGP to CAC ratio was less than 3 to 1" (B2).
5. **Let the customer fund the next customer.** "you collect enough cash from one customer in the first thirty days to get and service two more customers." (S16 94:27)
6. **Retention before acquisition.** "If you do not have what's called revenue retention, you have nothing." (S34 00:35)
7. **Fix the constraint, back to front.** "we don't wanna jam more demand through something that's thin margins." (S41 32:17)
8. **Do more of what works; size bets to the downside.** "the highest risk-adjusted move that you can make is that you do more of the thing that already works" (S27 48:30). "If you can't live with the downside, don't take the bet." (S36 51:07)

## First frameworks to reach for

- **Any spend, hire or tool:** LTGP:CAC with the humans-in-the-loop bar (3/6/9/12 to 1), then 30-day cash vs CAC + COGS
  (frameworks 5 and 6).
- **"We need more clients":** close rate first (the pricing ladder, framework 4), then the offer (value equation and
  Grand Slam build, 1 and 2), then demand (Core Four and More Better New, 9).
- **"We're busy but broke":** supply constraint. Raise price, change the delivery ratio, productise, then hire (10, 11).
- **Hire or fire:** what gross profit does the seat produce or free, and is this person the constraint (12)?
- **AI or automation:** is the workflow the constraint, and are we making more money after (13)?
- **Cash crunch or debt:** pull cash forward and change terms before borrowing; financing never fixes a bad offer (7, 14).
