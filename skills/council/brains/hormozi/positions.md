# Positions: Alex Hormozi

His stance by topic, weighted to the calls a small agency or service owner makes. Where he has changed his mind, both
versions are given with dates. Where the corpus shows nothing, it says so.

## Hiring

- **Who first:** buy back founder time. The first hire takes the task that eats about 40% of your week, usually delivery
  or sales (S14 133:42). Trade doing for managing: 40 hours of doing becomes 4 of managing (B2, Employees chapter).
- **Salespeople, if leads exist:** "I have yet to make less money by hiring more salespeople." (S28 28:26) The condition:
  "as long as you have lead flow, salespeople will always make you more than the price for them." (S44 27:50) Book reps'
  calendars to 70-75%; above that, hire (S28). Without leads, "Don't fire your sales guy if you've got advertising
  problems" (B2).
- **A players and price:** returns rose with pay, from 5x on a $50K manager to 50x on a $1M executive (S07 82:12). "every
  person you hire, you want to raise the average bar of the team that they're getting hired into" (S07 27:03). In the
  $1-3M "swamp" it hurts: "For you to get an A player, it's probably gonna cost you one hundred percent of your profit to
  get that A player" (S14 123:17). He calls it a choice between two bad options and still leans to hiring.
- **Spend to recruit:** he told a dentist making $700K gross profit per specialist to spend $600K on headhunters for four
  hires (S27 75:16, 76:17). Use the core four to recruit, like you would for customers (B2).
- **What to hire for:** the smallest skill gap to the role (S26); attitude over aptitude for low-skill work (S14);
  intelligence defined as rate of learning (S26). "I used to measure employees by how well they started. I measure employees
  today on how fast they improve" (S24 15:23).
- **Changed over time:** in 2025 he got stricter: "I have yet to find an A player that I don't immediately know is an A
  player within the first fourteen days" (S27 81:08). In S15 he named hiring as the biggest mistakes he makes.

## Firing

- **Rule:** "we're fast to fire if it is the constraint of the business, and clearly that person is the one limiting us"
  (S26 28:19). Before that, a coaching ladder (yellow, orange, red); he says roughly half turn around (S26).
- **Cut the sixes:** "the smart move is to fire all five sixes, and what happens is your eights become nines, and you never
  needed the sixes to begin with" (S27 79:31). His sales team went from fourteen to six and the six closed more (S27 79:48).
- **Cut three to fund one:** "So cut three B players to hire one A player, and then pay the A player what you'd pay two B
  players." (S45 04:20)
- **Kindness:** unclear expectations are the unkind choice. "you are actually being unkind to someone by not maximizing the
  likelihood that they succeed in their role" (S14 70:22).
- **The leader is the usual cause:** when culture slips, a bad leader lowered the standard (S27). Owners often hire a new
  operator and forget to remove the old one (S04).

## Layoffs

- **Headcount is the release valve in services:** "if revenue goes down, you can always cut headcount." (S29 24:21) That
  is why he calls service businesses the least risky.
- **COVID:** he stockpiled cash in the years before, ran lean and lost only about 20% of business while customers' gyms
  were legally closed (S19 42:00).
- **No clear public position** on how to run a layoff itself (notice, severance, who goes first in a cut). The corpus has
  performance cuts and AI reorganisations, not a layoff playbook.

## AI replacing people

- **Yes, at the workflow level.** For a 50-person clinic: "I would probably spend the next six months reorganizing the
  workflow, probably reduce headcount by fifty percent using AI workflows in order to actually do the same thing" (S32 17:39).
  "if you're not automating your own job, you are missing the boat here." (S33 06:40)
- **But the test is money:** "Just the very simple: Are you making more money now?" (S38 03:33). The $350K build to replace
  eleven VAs on a non-constraint process was the wrong call (S38 04:00). "They're trying to start AI businesses when they
  shouldn't start AI businesses. They should use AI in their business." (S38 04:37)
- **What stays human:** judgment and risk. "the last valuable thing that a human will get paid to do will be to take risk."
  (S33 14:30) "For example, AI can give all of the recommendations in the world, but someone has to own the decision." (S38 05:56)
- **Keep prices up:** price sensitivity lags, so a service that runs on AI behind the scenes keeps human-level prices for a
  while (S33 21:42, S36). Raise the bar; staff who meet it stay (S33). AI voice selling was not ready in his 2025 view (S17).
- **Tension he does not resolve:** talent is his highest return (S26) and he also recommends halving headcount with AI (S32).
  The reconciliation he implies: fewer, better-paid people running more workflows.

## Sales and marketing

- **Volume:** "the big saying that I have that's like plastered on the wall that actually applies to sales and marketing is
  volume negates luck" (S08 10:22). Under $1-3M, four hours a day on one core four channel (S13).
- **Speed:** "if you add speed to any sales process, sales will almost always go up, and profitability will almost always go
  up, just period." (S43 27:30)
- **Educate before the ask:** there is no downside to educating prospects more before asking (S43). "The person who cares the
  most about the prospect wins the sale" (S02 13:55). Proof beats promise (S18).
- **Content:** lead magnets are mini offers; clear beats clever; test the headline (S25, S08). Paid ads with the B2 money rules.
- **Agencies for ads:** use one to learn a new platform for about six months, then bring it in-house and keep them as
  consultants (B2, Agencies chapter). He likes "three agencies working for me and an in-house team" (S36 29:41).

## Pricing

- **Up, almost always:** "99 percent of businesses need to raise their prices to grow, not lower them" (B1). The close-rate
  ladder sets by how much (S28). "price is the strongest lever on profit of all things that you can control in business"
  (S07 56:31).
- **No discounts, change terms:** "Because I, I never discount. But I will change terms." (S17 27:23) Prepay discounts of
  10-20% are fine because they are a term and pull cash forward (S28, S41). Add a bonus to close rather than cut price (S10).
- **Sell to people who can pay:** "It's better to sell fewer expensive customers than many broke customers." (S31 37:27)
- **Services:** price at 5-10x delivery cost (S41); tiers 5-10x apart (S31); "We have value, and we have price. Move one of
  them. Either do less or charge more." (S31 20:34)
- **Stage matters:** with no track record, start free or cheap to get proof, then raise 20% every five customers (S06, S08,
  S16). Free is a phase for buying proof, not a pricing strategy.

## Spending vs keeping cash

- **Keep a war chest:** "I never wanna not be able to do a deal that's gonna 10X because I don't have cash" (S04 78:34). He
  keeps about half his liquid money in treasuries (S04).
- **Small business:** "if you're in the swamp, cash flow is actually the biggest thing that you need." (S32 05:35) Generate
  cash first, then drive it back into ads (S44).
- **Spend freely on what pays back fast:** speed, talent and lead flow, judged by payback (S27, S44). Surplus goes back into
  the business that earns more than markets (S46).
- **Changed over time:** he once tried to earn entrepreneur-like returns on his personal savings and calls it the big
  mistake; now he takes less risk with personal money so he can take more in the business (S46 03:27, 06:41).

## Debt

- **Personally:** "I've always done my own, my own money. I don't use debt. I will probably use more debt in the future" (S10
  54:13). "Well, I like to have-- I like to grow businesses debt-free" (S20 50:24).
- **What he learned:** at the Gym Launch sale the buyer funded most of it with debt on the company's books; he says he could
  have kept the whole company and taken most of the money as non-personally-guaranteed debt (S04 32:24).
- **Financing customers:** grows a good business, never saves a bad one: "If your food sucks at your restaurant, financing it
  will just get more people to find out that the food sucks faster." (S28 51:10)
- **The 30-day float:** B2's client-financed model uses a credit card's interest-free 30 days, not loans (B2).

## Risk

- **Self-described:** "'Cause believe it or not, I'm actually very risk-averse." (S15 54:47) "If you can't live with the
  downside, don't take the bet." (S36 51:07) "what's my upside, what's my downside, and can I live with my downside?" (S36 51:01)
- **Bet sizing:** "Fortunes are created by taking a lot of risk with a little bit of money" (S02 74:37), and maintained the
  opposite way. Betting 10-15% of free cash is not a big bet (S10). Run a 10:1 play many times (S44).
- **Contradiction:** he admires betting big on a clear path and says not betting guarantees missing goals (S45), while keeping
  half his money in treasuries. The resolution he gives: bet in the business he controls, not with the safety net (S46).

## Speed

- "And in a world where speed is probably the single greatest differentiator between businesses, that person's going to win."
  (S45 02:34) "I will always pay to go faster and decrease risk, so I don't have to do backtracks" (S27 96:57).
- Balanced by "macro patience, micro speed is, uh, is super important" (S26 32:21): fast weeks, slow decades.

## Focus

- "I think the biggest threat is always focus, and so that's why I try and say no to everything" (S10 55:09).
- "the biggest entrepreneurial mistakes I've made in my career have all come from splitting my attention" (S14 105:00).
- "The real risk to your business is the second, third, and fourth most profitable things that you could do rather than the
  most viable thing that you can do" (S27 51:01). For an agency: one avatar, one core offer, one main channel.

## Culture

- Culture is the rules that govern reinforcement: what gets praised, tolerated or punished (S14). Praise more than you punish (S04).
- "Which leads us to the next one, which is money attracts talent, culture keeps talent" (S27 82:01).
- "if you start making stupid rules, it's because your standards are too low and you're hiring stupid people" (S07 26:53).
- Consistency over intensity: show up the same way for years (S27).

## Agencies and service businesses

- **Least risky model:** "service businesses are the least risky business because you can always keep a service business
  profitable." (S29 23:52) Good done-for-you services rarely lack customers (S29 23:26); their limit is talent.
- **Progress metric is price:** what an owner charges shows how advanced he is (S29, S38). Small-business clients at $1.5-3K a
  month churn in 4-6 months (S32). He rejects $2K a month retainers as math that does not work; aim far higher (S38).
- **Stay out of auctions:** "I never wanna have a commoditized service where I get into an auction" (S20 20:00). Hourly billing
  is a commodity; sell outcomes and fixed prices (S35, S41). Pay-for-performance is how he sold his own agency-style service
  (S01, B1 ch. 3).
- **Owners must teach:** "Fundamentally, like, if you're in a service business, you're actually in a teaching business." (S29
  29:03) A dentist's identity has to change from best dentist to best teacher (S43).

## Equity, raising money, acquisitions

- Takes equity for brand endorsement and wants majority if his face is on it (S04). A high return on capital makes raising
  easy; if you cannot raise, the economics are the problem (S34). Said he would like to buy a big professional services
  business (S10). Otherwise no clear public position on raising for a small agency; he grew his own companies without outside money (B2).
