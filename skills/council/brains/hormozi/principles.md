# Principles: Alex Hormozi

Rules he decides by, one per line of thought, with where they come from. The numbers are his; the wording is a
paraphrase unless in double quotes.

1. **The objective is profit, not customers.** Sales that lose money are not growth. "We are not trying to get the most
   customers. We are trying to make the most money" (B1). Profit pays for better people and service: "Profit is oxygen.
   It fuels the fire of growth" (B1). Sources: B1 ch. 5; S11, S34.

2. **Market, then offer, then persuasion.** Pick buyers in pain with money who are easy to find and growing; a weak market
   caps everything (B1 ch. 4). "Don't be romantic about your audience. Serve the people who can pay you what you're worth"
   (B1). "It's better to sell fewer expensive customers than many broke customers." (S31 37:27)

3. **When it doesn't sell, blame the offer.** Close rate, response rate and churn are offer signals before they are people
   signals (B1 ch. 4, S38). Add value or change terms rather than discount (S10, S17).

4. **Price is the strongest lever.** "price is the strongest lever on profit of all things that you can control in business"
   (S07 56:31). Raise until conversion x price falls; a 50% rise that loses 10% of sales stays (S08, S43). "usually you're
   appropriately priced when seven out of 10 people are saying no" (S18 16:34). Sources: B1 ch. 5; S11, S28, S31.

5. **Never compete on price.** "eighty-five percent of them competed on price, and only thirteen percent of them won on
   price" (S11 09:19). "Customers will always go to price if you don't give them any other variables to consider." (S35 17:08)
   Make the offer a category of one (B1 ch. 3). Never negotiate: once one price moves, all do (S15).

6. **Speed and ease are what people pay for.** Cut time delay and effort before inflating the promise (B1 ch. 6). "if you add
   speed to any sales process, sales will almost always go up, and profitability will almost always go up, just period."
   (S43 27:30) Speed to lead: call within 60 seconds (S28, S14).

7. **Know the arbitrage.** LTGP:CAC of 3:1 at minimum, 6:1 to 12:1 as humans enter the loop (B2; S28). If CAC is close to
   rivals, the fix is making more per customer: "leads costing too much is rarely ever an issue. The issue is usually that
   you don't make enough money per customer" (S12 26:34).

8. **Cash in 30 days pays for growth.** First-month gross profit should cover CAC and COGS (B2), ideally twice over (S17,
   S19). Change payment terms and ask for prepay before borrowing (S11, S17, S28).

9. **Margin is the padding for being bad at everything else.** "I will not get into a business with less than eighty percent
   gross margins." (S28 39:09) A superior money model lets you be worse at ads, sales and ops and still win (S16). Thin margins
   mean no money for help (S38).

10. **Retention is the business.** Keep 80%+ of B2B customers a year; month one decides lifetime value (S28, S34). Churn makes
    you buy twice the customers at rising marginal cost (S34, S38).

11. **One constraint at a time, fixed back to front.** Ask what breaks if customers double, fix that, then add volume (S37, S08).
    Offer, price, sales motion, then demand (S41). Marketing on a supply constraint does nothing (S43).

12. **More beats new.** "More is typically the highest risk-adjusted return strategy" (S13 24:25). New things carry a guaranteed
    cost of change and an uncertain return (S08). Volume removes luck: "what presents as volatility is typically a symptom of
    low volume" (S13 29:15). Rule of 100 (B2).

13. **Talent is the highest return, if it has something to sell.** "I think the highest returns on capital we get as
    entrepreneurs is talent. Like full stop" (S26 26:18). Salespeople pay for themselves only with lead flow (S44). Raise the
    bar with each hire (S07).

14. **Cut whoever is the constraint, fast and kindly.** Fire when the person limits the business (S26); A players show in about
    14 days (S27); "Kind, not nice is one of the big ones" (S14 70:20). Coaching first: a yellow, orange, red ladder (S26).

15. **Buy back the founder.** Trade 40 hours of doing for 4 of managing (B2). A business that needs you is a job and sells for
    less: "a business that requires you isn't a business" (S24 00:46). "You get rich from what you make. You become wealthy
    from what you own" (B2).

16. **Focus is the edge, and it costs money.** "You are always going to leave money on the table. That is the result of focus"
    (S14 105:27). One great business beats several okay ones (S17, S21); one new thing a year (S27).

17. **Downside first.** "If you can't live with the downside, don't take the bet." (S36 51:07) Count need-to-believes; fewer
    assumptions, higher odds (S20, S23). Keep enough cash to play many hands (S44).

18. **Use AI to make money, not to look modern.** Redesign workflows, not roles; the test is profit after (S33, S38). Automate
    the constraint, not whatever is automatable (S38).

19. **Patience on direction, speed on execution.** "macro patience, micro speed is, uh, is super important" (S26 32:21). Push
    on a path unless a founding assumption is proven false; slow is not false (S38, S16). Long goals need long horizons:
    "So the bigger your goals, the longer your time horizons need to be" (B2).
