# Stories: Alex Hormozi

The cases he retells, with his numbers and the lesson he draws. Use them as proof in a card, paraphrased; never present
them as his words unless in double quotes with an id.

1. **The first gym paid its rent and nothing else.** "I made exactly $4,972 my first month of my gym. My rent was $4,972"
   (S05 134:53). The first 29 members came from pity before opening (S05). Later he presold memberships so customers funded
   the next gym: "it cost me almost no money out of pocket because I let my customers pay for my gym for me" (S11 17:08).
   **Lesson:** get paid before you deliver; customer cash is the cheapest capital there is.

2. **Turnarounds that did not scale.** He flew around the country running more than thirty gym turnarounds in under two
   years (S29 18:30). Paid on performance, the more he sold the more refund risk he carried: "this was the only model I knew
   and so the more I sold, the more refund risk I exposed myself to" (S10 10:08). He moved to teaching owners how to fill
   their gyms instead of doing it for them (S10). **Lesson:** a model that grows your risk with every sale has to change shape.

3. **Gym Launch: the 100 to 1 window.** "The next arbitrage that occurred was in Gym Launch, where we got a hundred to one for
   the first year. I spent a hundred grand and made ten million dollars" (S07 87:52). Zero to $2.2M a month in 20 months
   (S17). Priced at $16K, about 3x the top competitor; 35% later bought a $42K-a-year upgrade (B1 ch. 5). **Lesson:** a
   premium offer to a narrow, starving market prints cash; when the window opens, spend into it.

4. **Stuck at $30-40M.** "I was at thirty to forty million a year for three years for Gym Launch, and the big aha that I had
   was that I needed to build a brand" (S27 53:40). **Lesson:** a plateau can be a market-size or brand ceiling, not an
   effort problem; his peers had markets 10-100x his (S04).

5. **The sale he would structure differently.** The buyer of his companies funded most of the deal with debt on the
   business's own books and pulled the money back out (S04). "I could've kept the whole fucking thing and gotten ninety percent
   of the money not personally guaranteed and de-risked myself" (S04 32:24). **Lesson:** learn how the other side finances a
   deal before you sell; non-recourse debt can de-risk you without selling.

6. **The wrong software.** He told the story of building lead-nurture software for gyms: "simply made a CRM for gyms rather than
   a lead nurture software for gyms, that company would have sold for half a billion dollars" (S23 54:17). "I literally allocated
   capital to build technology, and I built the wrong one." (S17 51:47) His wife Leila had warned the timing was early: "And so,
   like she was right, you know?" (S21 06:21). **Lesson:** a second venture while the first is working costs focus, and the
   product must sit where the customer's money already flows.

7. **ALAN: zero to $1.7M a month.** "And, like, Alan went from zero to, you know, one point seven million a month within six
   months." (S17 06:50) A top ad was "four reasons why you'll never have a million-dollar agency" (S25 11:53). **Lesson:** name
   the pain directly; the pain is the pitch.

8. **COVID with a war chest.** Running hard for years before, they had stockpiled cash, ran lean and lost about 20% of business
   while customers' gyms were legally shut and rivals went under (S19 42:00). **Lesson:** cash reserves are what let you keep
   going when the market closes.

9. **Fourteen reps down to six.** One rep was stealing and others knew; he cut the team from fourteen to six and the six closed
   more deals than the fourteen (S27 79:41). **Lesson:** low performers drag the good ones down; cut them and the eights become
   nines.

10. **The dentist who would not pay for talent.** Each specialist dentist made about $700K gross profit a year; he told the owner
    to spend $600K on headhunters to land four of them, even if it cut that year's profit from $4.6M to $4M (S27 75:16, 76:17).
    **Lesson:** price a hire against the gross profit it produces, not against this year's P&L.

11. **The $350K AI build.** A company spent "three hundred and fifty thousand dollars to try and create an AI system that would
    replace what these eleven virtual assistants were doing" (S38 04:00), about $11K a month of VAs, on a step that was not the
    constraint. **Lesson:** automate what limits growth; could is not should.

12. **Money Models launch.** $106M in sales in 72 hours (S21). "the book about monetization made 100 million in a weekend, so
    like, that's how you know the stuff inside of it works" (S21 89:01). He hired four directors 90 days before to add capacity
    (S27) and still lost money on the books themselves, even prepaid (S27). **Lesson:** the front end can lose money if the
    sequence of offers behind it pays.

13. **Frustrated Fred and Wealthy William.** Both make $2M a year in profit. Fred runs the business himself and banks about
    $500K a year after tax and living costs. William has a team that runs it without him, so it trades at 6x profit, $12M.
    When both add $500K of profit, Fred saves $250K more; William's business gains $3M in value (S24 02:42). **Lesson:**
    owner-free profit is worth a multiple; profit that needs you is just income, taxed.

14. **The chiro who thought he had saturated.** A $2M-a-year owner spending about $30K a month on one ad platform, with no
    content and no outreach, said he had saturated a $15.1B industry; another chiropractor in the room had spent $30K across
    four platforms the week before (B2, More Better New). **Lesson:** saturation is almost always under-volume.

15. **The theme park in 90 days.** Asked to save a failing park before its bad season, his first move: "So the first thing that we
    probably have to do is generate cash." (S44 05:16) Then pull cash forward and pour it back into ads (S44). **Lesson:** in a
    cash crisis, sell now with prepay and passes before any long-term fix.

16. **The first $100K.** In B1's execution section he shows a bank balance of $101,018 and says it is still the richest he has
    ever felt, tens of millions later (B1, "Your First $100,000"). **Lesson:** the first cushion buys freedom of choice; the rest is less felt.
