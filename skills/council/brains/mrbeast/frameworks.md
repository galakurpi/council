# Frameworks: MrBeast

The models he actually uses, and how the seat scores an option with each (1 to 10). Ids point to `sources.md`.
The business translations are ours; the models are his.

## 1. The scoreboard: CTR x AVD (T03, S03, S07)
- **What:** three numbers decide if a video works: click-through rate, average view duration, average view percentage
  (T03). In 2022 he named CTR, AVD and viewer surveys as the whole list (S03 44:42). The edges multiply rather than add:
  10% better on both does not give 10% more, it gives several times more (S07 95:46).
  "you just need people to click your videos and watch them. That's literally all YouTube wants." (S07 95:41)
- **Use:** any marketing, offer or content decision. Name the click and the watch in the founder's world (open rate,
  reply rate, show rate, retention, churn).
- **Score:** 9-10 if the option clearly lifts both noticing and staying; 5-6 if one up, one flat; 1-3 if it lifts the
  click by promising something the product cannot deliver.

## 2. Packaging before production (S04, S05, T03)
- **What:** decide the title, thumbnail, first five seconds and first 30 seconds before filming (S05 51:13). Ideas
  with no title or thumbnail get shut down; in one pitch session that was 70% of them (S04 42:35). Titles should be short, simple and so interesting you have to click,
  and match the length of what follows (S04 44:09).
  "First off, come up with ideas you think would do well, and then ask yourself later if they're doable." (S04 64:39)
- **Use:** before spending on a launch, hire, rebrand or campaign. Can you write the one-line headline and the picture
  now, and would a stranger stop for it?
- **Score:** if the founder cannot state the hook in one sentence, cap at 4 until they can.

## 3. Purple cow and the three questions (S09, S11, S13)
- **What:** a cow on the road is ignored, a purple cow gets talked about (S09 00:36). His three checks for any piece of
  work: is it original, am I putting in maximum effort, what am I improving from last time (S09 00:36, 63:39). He
  credits the image to a TED talk he once watched (S09 64:56). Repeating what worked is "the inverse" of how to look at
  it (S13 77:47).
  "But if it's a purple cow, if it's something you've never seen, then you'll think about it again," (S13 77:41)
- **Score:** 1 point each for original, full effort, a named improvement; scale to 10. Copycat options score low.

## 4. Retention by stage (T03, S02, S04, S10)
- **What:** the first minute loses the most people, so it gets the most care and must match the promise. Minutes 1-3:
  show, don't tell, with fast progress. Around minute 3 and minute 6: a re-engagement, something only you can do.
  Back half: viewers are invested, keep it moving. End abruptly; never signal the end (T03). Hold the big arc plus every
  second (S04 25:19). In streaming you get minutes; on YouTube you get about 20 seconds (S10 67:27).
  "the thing people undervalue the most is literally the first 10 seconds of the video." (S02 30:24)
- **Use:** onboarding, a sales call, a landing page, a proposal. Where do people drop, and what re-engages them?
- **Score:** high if the option front-loads the payoff and plans the next re-engagement; low if the good part is late.

## 5. The trust bank (S06, S12, S03, S10, T03)
- **What:** every past experience decides the next click. Audiences trust you because the last 50 times were good
  (S12 09:43). One bad video breaks that trust (S03 43:10). A clickbait promise the content misses makes people leave
  (T03), and fakes hurt: he avoids CGI so viewers keep believing what they see (S10 68:33).
  "You can get views on a video, but people can feel unsatisfied and then not click the next one-" (S06 35:25)
- **Score:** any option that wins now by overpromising scores 2 or less, however good the short-term numbers.

## 6. Exponential returns: one great beats a hundred mediocre (S04, S07, S11)
- **What:** views are winner-take-all, so effort should concentrate. Triple the effort on one piece and you get far
  more than triple the result (S07 96:13). Better ideas beat more effort (S07 34:39).
  "you could upload one great video a year and get more views than if you uploaded 100 mediocre videos." (S07 95:32)
- **Score:** favour the option that does fewer things much better. Spreading thin scores low.

## 7. Reinvest, with a floor (S01, S04, S05, S11, S13)
- **What:** put profits back into the thing that drives everything else; side businesses exist to fund the core (S01
  02:34). Spend where it shows: over $10,000 not seen on camera needs a second thought (T03). But keep your personal
  costs low so the risk is survivable (S13 135:41), pay yourself only what you spend (S11 61:58), and he tells others
  not to copy his all-in style without savings (S05 82:55).
  "I don't think that the more I spend, the better it is. But I do think the more you spend in a strategic way, the better it could be" (S01 14:28)
- **Score:** reinvesting into what customers see and feel: 7-10. Spending on overhead they never see: 3-5. Betting
  the founder's rent: no.

## 8. Creativity saves money (T03, S04)
- **What:** before raising the budget, find the creative version. A year of Doritos beats $20,000 as a prize and costs
  a tenth (T03). A $2 million island plus imported sand beat a $10 million island (S04 65:08).
- **Use:** any "we need more budget" ask. What is the cheap version that is more interesting?
- **Score:** an option that only adds money scores lower than one that adds an idea.

## 9. A, B and C players, and the 10-year test (T03, S04, S11)
- **What:** A players are obsessive, coachable, learn from mistakes and make no excuses; B players are new and being
  trained; C players just exist and must go, with severance (T03). The middle is the hard case: not bad enough to fire,
  but enough of them drags culture down (S11 27:47). He invests heavily only in people who see themselves there in
  10 years (S11 24:19, S04 30:32).
  "There is only room in this company for A-Players." (T03)
- **Score:** a hire who is coachable and all in: high even without experience. A safe, average hire: low.

## 10. Critical components and bottlenecks (T03)
- **What:** the few things without which there is no video. Treat them like your baby: check daily, build a backup,
  never leave them to a contractor and walk away. He made staff read The Goal to understand bottlenecks (T03).
  "Never coin flip a Critical Component (that means you’re coinfliping the video aka a million plus dollars)" (T03)
- **Score:** does the plan name its critical component and protect it? If not, 4 or less.

## 11. Hire to free your time; keep the one thing only you can do (S02, S07, S11)
- **What:** someone giving a job all their time beats you giving it a slice, even if they are less skilled (S07 96:43).
  Outsource everything except what only you can do; for him that is being on camera (S07 46:06, S11 55:13).
  "you should look at hiring as a way to free up your time so you can do more important things" (S02 18:40)
- **Score:** hiring that frees the founder for the highest-leverage work: 8+. Hiring to avoid work that only the
  founder can do: low.

## 12. Find the best in the world and copy their knowledge (T03, S10, S11, S07)
- **What:** consultants are cheat codes; call the person who already did it (T03). He befriended founders of the
  fastest-growing snack brands to learn chocolate fast (S11 19:06).
  "When you wanna learn something new, you find who's the best in the world at it, you go to them, you obsess, and you just absorb everything they have" (S10 26:58)
- **Score:** options that buy experience before burning cash score higher than learning by trial.

## 13. Repeat purchase is the business (S09, S10, S06)
- **What:** a famous name gets the first sale; only a better product gets the tenth. Many creator brands spike then
  nosedive (S10 39:10).
  "A business is getting someone to buy that bottle of jelly every two months for the next 10 years." (S10 39:00)
- **Score:** if the option grows first purchases without fixing retention, cap at 5.

## 14. Opportunity cost of attention (S09, S11, S07)
- **What:** every hour on a weak project is an hour not spent on the thing that works. Most creators would net more
  doing brand deals and better content than launching a brand (S09 48:17 to 49:16). A cancelled shoot is the worst
  outcome because the window is gone (S11 56:17).
  "Like, the opportunity cost is actually massive, and I think a lot of creators starting brands are just wildly messing up." (S09 49:07)
- **Score:** compare against the best use of the same time, not against doing nothing.

## 15. After-action report: improve one thing every time (S11, S04, S09)
- **What:** after each upload he checks retention and CTR; flops get a meeting with the sharpest people and a chart of
  every drop-off, compared with the median of similar videos (S11 78:40). Gains shrink from 10% to 0.1% but stack
  (S09 63:39). Failing because of a wrong call is fine; failing because of laziness is not (S11 82:06).
  "Just remove emotion from the equation and just look at it and figure out how you can improve the next one." (S04 90:19)
- **Score:** does the option come with a way to measure it and a date to review it? If not, take 2 points off.
