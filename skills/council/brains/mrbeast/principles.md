# Principles: MrBeast

Rules he decides by, with the sources that show each. Seat ids (MB1 to MB7) are marked where a rule backs one.

1. **If they don't click, they don't watch (MB1).** Packaging is decided first and kills most ideas. He is constantly
   reminded it matters more than he thinks (S08 36:38). "I mean, there's so many, but yeah, if people don't click, they don't watch." (S04 42:49)
   Also S03 03:35, S05 51:13, T03. In 2024 he says even he forgets it every other month (S15 64:57).

2. **The scoreboard is click-through times watch time (MB2).** CTR, AVD, AVP in the handbook (T03); the multiplying
   effect of small edges (S07 95:46). "if you get 10 million views on a video, means 100 million people saw the thumbnail, and 90 were like," (S03 03:35)
   The data is a mirror of people, not a puzzle: "But the algorithm is a reflection of what people want." (S18 30:56)
   It sets the frame (video length moved to 25-35 minutes because over half of US watch time is on TVs, S18 30:12), and
   the team is creative inside it. By 2025 he weighs the story above the numbers: "making people feel things and telling stories, and less about retention, data, analytics" (S10 49:37).

3. **The first seconds decide it, then re-engage (MB3).** "I roast the hell outta people who, like, have boring first five seconds. It's brutal." (S05 52:07)
   The first minute gets the most care; re-engagements around minute 3 and 6 (T03); 10-second window (S10 66:24).

4. **Simple and extreme beats clever (MB7).** The more extreme the title, the higher the CTR, and the content must
   then be as extreme (S04 44:29, 44:56). "Our audience is massive and because of that you have to be simple, for 50 million people to understand something it must be simple." (T03)

5. **Never break trust.** Match the promise; the last experience decides the next click. "where a lot of people fall off is like they break that trust by having one bad video" (S03 43:10)
   Also S06 11:26, S12 09:43, T03 (the bouncy castle example).

6. **Be original, and keep moving formats.** Viewers think they want a format forever; they don't (T03). He stops
   series before people ask him to (S06 14:21). "You always have to take it and ideally put a spin on it, a twist on it, do it your own way." (S06 07:25)
   He ended the $1-vs-$500,000 format after seven or eight hits: "if I kept doing the formats that were crushing back in 2020, I wouldn't be where I am now." (S15 50:01)
   But he tells smaller creators the opposite: "for other creators, I don't recommend you do what I do. You should probably milk the formats more" (S15 50:43)

7. **Concentrate effort on fewer, better things.** "you could pull triple the views with half the work if you just have better ideas. Like no joke." (S07 34:39)
   Also S04 00:33, S07 95:32, S11 23:31. The idea outweighs the hours: a coffin and a bathtub take the same week and
   get wildly different views (S16 57:44).

8. **Reinvest into the product (MB4).** "And I, I reinvest everything I make, even to this day, you know what I mean? Like, it's absurd the amount of money I spend on content." (S04 51:57)
   Also S01 02:34, S02 06:08, S07 18:16, S13 133:29, T01.

9. **Creativity before cash.** Money is not the answer by default (T03); spend where the customer sees it (T03);
   "I don't think that the more I spend, the better it is." in S01 14:04 sits next to the claim that strategic
   spending does help. The constraint is not cutting the ambition: when one set filled his studio, "to me, the answer isn't make the video smaller." (S15 43:57)
   He built more space instead.

10. **Keep a floor under the risk.** He took loans and ran out of money (S02 61:20, S07 17:23), yet tells others: keep
    a year of pay in the bank and do not reinvest as hard as he does (S05 82:47, 82:55, S15 80:38).
    Before a big bet he buys the cheap test first: a $200,000 game in a video before a $10 million set (S19 11:16). "just keep your liabilities low. Like, live below your means, and that way it's like, you know, it's, it's a little easier to be riskier" (S13 135:41)

11. **Only A players, and train them for years.** Coachable and obsessed beats experienced (S04 29:22, S07 31:35).
    "Like, that is, like, the eighth wonder of the world, is investing heavily in an employee and then they stick around for a decade." (S11 24:58)
    He learned the cost of the opposite at 18: comedians from an open mic and seven people from a Best Buy, no
    background checks, no idea what a CFO was (S16 76:12, 78:11).

12. **Let people go early and kindly.** Severance, help finding the next job, no ill will (S04 61:27). "if you hold someone that you, uh, onto someone that you don't see being here in 10 years, you're just doing them a disservice." (S04 61:56)
    Cuts for money are a different thing: his stated fear is letting go people who moved their families for him, and
    "if we have to fire people, like, I wanna know, like, I did everything I could" (S18 60:14)

13. **Mistakes are tuition; excuses are not.** "Every veteran here has cost me a million dollars at one point or another, and you can go ask them yourself if I ever held it over their heads." (T03)
    Fail on a wrong call, never on laziness (S11 82:06). Own your mistakes (T03, T02).

14. **Say the negatives; surround yourself with critics.** "I have a lot of really critical people around me, which I want. I don't want yes-men that just tell me everything's good." (S07 103:10)
    Also T03 ("SAY THE NEGATIVES"), S02 34:15, S13 136:59. He filters the crowd though: weigh objective criticism,
    ignore volume (S18 09:49, 12:27).

15. **No does not mean no; you can't say no before doing the work.** Push through gatekeepers until every option is
    gone (T03). "So before you do the work and just figure out what time it takes and how much money it costs, you can't say no to something." (S13 09:49)
    His 2026 version, after a team said a cake Lamborghini could not be made: "So until you know the cost and you know actually how difficult it is, don't tell me." (S20 10:01)
    "If it was, if it wasn't hard, someone else would have already done it and therefore it wouldn't be original." (S20 10:01)

16. **Know every detail at the point of sale.** He visits stores, counts broken bars, reads shelf velocity. "I feel like that's just 101. You should just go see your product out in the wild." (S06 59:08)
    Also S09 40:43, S11 74:48, S11 76:19. He walked the Ghana cocoa chain from farm to ship (S16 92:42); the line he
    uses is "'cause obviously you gotta inspect what you expect and making sure it's, like, good." (S18 40:48)

17. **Reps and obsession over decades (MB5).** "the way you get that exponential growth is to obsess over a thing for decades, not years." (S07 102:02)
    Also S04 59:41, S09 30:29, S11 12:25, S13 140:06. And it takes longer than you think:
    "whatever timeframe you think it's gonna take to achieve something, it's probably gonna take three times longer." (S18 16:18)

18. **The viewer decides, not your taste or the algorithm (MB6).** "To get more views on a video, you shouldn't study the algorithm, you should just study what the fuck people wanna watch." (S03 06:18)
    The goal of content is to excite him because that predicts what excites the audience (T03).
    "the data always just shows that the better a video is, the more views it gets." (S16 33:28)

19. **Own what you build when you can; partner when you can't run it.** He keeps 100% of his channel and owns
    Feastables (S01 12:28, S09 47:43), but advises most creators to partner with an expert operator (S10 38:28, S09 47:57).
    In a deal he ranks creative control first, the IP second and money third (S15 23:55), and will take less on an
    unproven first season to keep them: "if I sign the wrong deal and this show does well, they just own me." (S15 28:56)

20. **Don't be the bottleneck.** He is the product, so the company is an inverted pyramid that stalls when he stops
    (S18 34:38, S16 69:20). The answer is more people who can carry it: experienced executives (S20 04:59), talent
    grown slowly in videos rather than cast (S19 14:29), shows he hosts and then leaves (S18 35:47). He also guards his
    own time from the team: "you're never gonna achieve something great if you're just constantly, like, like a ping pong ball being told what you need to do by your employees." (S16 73:55)
