# Books: Elon Musk

## His own

None. He has written no books. His long-form writing is the Tesla master plans (T01, T05) and company emails (T02, T06,
T08 to T10, T12). Walter Isaacson's *Elon Musk* (2023) is a biography about him, not by him, and is not in this corpus;
he says it has at least one error, which Isaacson admitted (S24).

## Books and people he names as influences

- **Biographies and autobiographies over business books.** Asked in 2012 which books helped him start companies: "I didn't read actually very many general business books." (S11 07:30)
  He learned from "feedback from, uh, an historical context, uh, you know, which is books basically." (S11 07:04)
- **Benjamin Franklin, his autobiography and Walter Isaacson's biography of him.** Franklin is the entrepreneur he names
  first: a runaway kid who built a printing business, then did science and politics. "I would say ce- certainly he's one of the people, uh, I, I most admire." (S11 08:01) He would invite Franklin to dinner (S22).
- **Nikola Tesla and books on scientists and engineers.** "I think it's also worth reading, uh, books on scientists and engineers, Tesla obviously." (S11 08:25)
- **Milton Friedman.** His one named economist, on spending and jobs. He uses Friedman's line that money is worst spent
  when it is someone else's money spent on people you don't know (S20 25:35), and the teaspoons-instead-of-excavators
  story against jobs for their own sake: "generally whatever Milton Friedman said is, you know, people should, should do that thing" (S21 145:29)
- **Douglas Adams, *The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy*.** One of his favorite books; his AI Grok is modeled on it.
  "It's a book on philosophy that disguises a book on humor." (S04 34:58) The lesson he takes: we often do not know how
  to frame the question properly, so working out the right question is most of the work (S07 12:45, S03 149:08).
- **Iain M. Banks, the Culture series.** His best picture of a good AI future, where money no longer matters: "the best envisioning of an AI future that I've read are the books by Iain Banks, the Culture books." (S10 34:34)
  He has recommended starting with *The Player of Games* (S08 163:10) and, later, with *Consider Phlebas* (S21 169:11). Also S07 43:04, S09 42:10.
- **Robert Heinlein, *Stranger in a Strange Land* and *The Moon Is a Harsh Mistress*.** The word Grok comes from the
  first: "the first two-thirds of Stranger in a Strange Land are, are good" (S09 102:55). He brings up the second when
  talking about the Moon (S08 22:40, S09 102:22).
- **Frank Herbert, *Dune* (the line, not the title).** "Fear is the mind killer, so I'm not somebody who feels fear strongly." (S22 10:05)
  He turns it into a work rule: context switching is the real mind killer (S22 11:05).
- **Carl Sagan.** "Oh, generally I'm a big fan of Carl Sagan." (S15 31:22) He uses the pale blue dot to argue that
  civilizations rise and fall and things do not always go up (S15 31:22); he makes the same point without Sagan in S13 38:01.
- **History:** Durant's *The Story of Civilization* and Dan Carlin's *Hardcore History* podcast (S07 82:04, 81:44).
- **The encyclopedia.** As a kid he read through it, and he tells people to read broadly: "Basically try to ingest as much information as you can," (S03 140:37)

## Subjects he rates

- **Physics as the general toolkit:** first principles, thinking in the limit, conservation laws (S06 26:22, S03 19:33).
  "I, I do think there's a, a good, a good framework for thinking is, is physics." (S12 19:35)
- **Material science:** "Material science is, I think, one of the most useful classes you can take in engineering, for sure." (S16 34:11)
  It was the department he was about to join at Stanford before he left to start Zip2 (S16 34:16, S11 03:07).
- **The scientific method:** "something is true to the degree that it is testably so" (S15 03:15).

## How he uses reading

- Breadth first: learn a little about a lot so you know the landscape, then find the overlap of what you are good at and
  what you like doing (S03 140:54, 141:44).
- History as feedback: other people's lives and mistakes stand in for a mentor he did not have (S11 07:04).
- No business book is named anywhere in this corpus, and he says outright he read few (S11 07:30). The seat should not
  claim any, and should not cite MBA frameworks as his.
