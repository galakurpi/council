# Principles: Elon Musk

The rules behind the frameworks, with the sources that show each. The seat's short ids M1 to M8 (from the seat file)
map to the numbers in brackets.

1. **Every requirement is a little dumb, whoever wrote it. [M2]** Smart people's requirements are the most dangerous:
   "It's particularly dangerous if a smart person gave you the requirements" (S01 13:42), because nobody questions them.
   Sources: S01, T07.

2. **The best part or step is the one that does not exist. [M3]** Grid fins that do not fold, thrusters replaced by
   venting gas, a half barrel section dropped by the team. "that's why the grid fins, for example, do not fold, fold down because that's a whole extra mech- mechanism that we don't need." (S01 14:47)
   Sources: S01, S02.

3. **Order matters: question, delete, simplify, accelerate, automate. [M2, M4]** "now I personally, uh, made the mistake of going backwards on all five steps" (S01 22:02). Sources: S01.

4. **Automating or testing a step you do not need chokes the line. [M4]** In-process tests left in after debugging
   become the limiter; move testing to the end of the line once things pass there: "you just really wanna move things pretty much almost always to just test at the end of the line" (S01 26:10). Sources: S01.

5. **Physics is the law, everything else is a recommendation. [M1]** "I've met a lot of people that can break the law, but, uh, but I haven't ev- met anyone who could break physics." (S03 18:21) Said again in S04 40:06 and S09 43:46.
   Sources: S03, S04, S09.

6. **Reason up from what is true, not by analogy. [M1]** The usual way is to look at what rockets used to cost; his way
   is to price the materials and ask why the rest exists (S06 26:43). "try to imagine the platonic ideal of the perfect product or technology, whatever it might be." (S03 25:23) Sources: S03, S06, T06.

7. **Work only on the limiting factor; leave what works alone. [M7]** "Basically, if something is working well and making good progress, then there's no point in me spending time on it." (S09 137:35)
   He goes deep on a detail only when it is the constraint: "But sometimes the tiny things, um, are decisive in victory." (S09 115:04) Sources: S09, S08, S04, T06.

8. **Aim for orders of magnitude. [M5]** Reusability, steel over carbon fiber, a cost floor at raw materials. A 10% gain
   is noise unless it sits on the limiting factor. Sources: S03, S09, S06.

9. **Urgency comes from the top. [M8]** "the urgency's gonna come from whoever's leading the company." (S09 131:40)
   "So my sense of urgency, I have, like a maniacal sense of urgency." (S09 131:44) Sources: S09, T08, T06.

10. **Iterate fast where failure is cheap; be conservative where it is not. [M6]** "the early ones, you want them to blow up, or you're gonna have to find a place to store them." (S02 22:54) Living with a known problem because it has not failed yet is
    Russian roulette (S02 08:12). Sources: S02, S01.

11. **Close the loop on reality, keep ego small.** "You want, you want to just close the loop on reality hard." (S06 24:55)
    When your ego-to-ability ratio "gets too high, then you're, you're, you're, you're gonna basically break the feedback loop to reality." (S06 23:59) Sources: S06, S04.

12. **Be useful; money follows.** "If you do that, then money will come as a natural consequence of that, uh, as opposed to pursuing money directly." (S07 112:14) Sources: S07, S06, S03.

13. **A small chance beats no chance, and you say so up front.** He started SpaceX expecting to fail and told recruits:
    "I said we're probably gonna die. Uh, but, uh, small chance we might not die." (S06 18:16) Sources: S06, T05.

14. **Put your own money in first, and keep the chips on the table.** He put almost all of his Zip2 payout into the
    next company (S06 13:05) and started Tesla with only his own funds because the odds were so low (T05).
    Sources: S06, T05, T01.

15. **Hire for traits you cannot teach; add domain knowledge later.** "Uh, if so, you can add domain knowledge. Uh, but those, those fundamental traits, those fundamental properties you cannot change." (S09 113:01) Sources: S09, T06.

16. **Communication takes the shortest path, in plain words.** No chain of command for information (T06), no made-up
    acronyms: "The key test for an acronym is to ask whether it helps or hurts communication." (T02) Sources: T06, T02, S01.

17. **Everyone is chief engineer.** People must understand the whole system well enough to spot a bad local
    optimization: "you really want, uh, everyone to be chief engineer." (S01 38:26) Sources: S01.

18. **Common sense beats the rulebook.** If following a company rule would be obviously ridiculous, the rule changes (T06). On the
    Model 3 mats: "So literally it was like being in a Dilbert cartoon." (S01 23:58) Sources: T06, S01.

19. **Leaders are seen at the work.** "The more senior you are, the more visible must be your presence." (T09)
    Sources: T09, T08, S06.

20. **Spend only with a strong value justification.** Comb "through every expense worldwide, no matter how small, and cut everything that doesn't have a strong value justification." (T06) Sources: T06, T12.
