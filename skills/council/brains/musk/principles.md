# Principles: Elon Musk

The rules behind the frameworks, with the sources that show each. The seat's short ids M1 to M12 (from the seat file)
map to the numbers in brackets; M10 is also framework 13, M12 framework 14.

1. **Every requirement is a little dumb, whoever wrote it. [M2, M9]** Smart people's requirements are the most dangerous:
   "It's particularly dangerous if a smart person gave you the requirements" (S01 13:42), because nobody questions them.
   A requirement needs a person who can explain it, not a department (S17 11:01). Sources: S01, S17, T07.

2. **The best part or step is the one that does not exist. [M3]** Grid fins that do not fold, thrusters replaced by
   venting gas, a half barrel section dropped by the team, 300 robots gone with one casting. "that's why the grid fins, for example, do not fold, fold down because that's a whole extra mech- mechanism that we don't need." (S01 14:47)
   "this sounds insanely obvious, but we've deleted so many parts from the car that were, did nothing" (S17 12:00)
   Sources: S01, S02, S16, S17.

3. **Order matters: question, delete, simplify, accelerate, automate. [M2, M4]** "now I personally, uh, made the mistake of going backwards on all five steps" (S01 22:02). He still tells the story in 2023 so others skip the mistake (S17 13:05). Sources: S01, S17.

4. **Automating or testing a step you do not need chokes the line. [M4]** In-process tests left in after debugging
   become the limiter; move testing to the end of the line once things pass there: "you just really wanna move things pretty much almost always to just test at the end of the line" (S01 26:10). Sources: S01.

5. **Physics is the law, everything else is a recommendation. [M1]** "I've met a lot of people that can break the law, but, uh, but I haven't ev- met anyone who could break physics." (S03 18:21) Said again in S04 40:06 and S09 43:46. His test for an idea the team calls crazy:
   "There's nothing that's preventing it from happening from a physics standpoint." (S23 40:46) Sources: S03, S04, S09, S23.

6. **Reason up from what is true, not by analogy. [M1]** Most of life runs on analogy, "which essentially means kinda copying what other people do with slight variations." (S12 19:35) That is fine for daily life and wrong for anything new.
   The usual way is to look at what rockets used to cost; his way is to price the materials and ask why the rest exists
   (S06 26:43). "try to imagine the platonic ideal of the perfect product or technology, whatever it might be." (S03 25:23) Sources: S03, S06, S11, S12, T06.

7. **Work only on the limiting factor; leave what works alone. [M7]** "Basically, if something is working well and making good progress, then there's no point in me spending time on it." (S09 137:35)
   He goes deep on a detail only when it is the constraint: "But sometimes the tiny things, um, are decisive in victory." (S09 115:04) Sources: S09, S08, S04, S17, T06.

8. **Aim for orders of magnitude. [M5]** Reusability, steel over carbon fiber, a cost floor at raw materials, a tunnel
   that costs a tenth as much. A 10% gain is noise unless it sits on the limiting factor. Sources: S03, S09, S06, S13.

9. **Urgency comes from the top. [M8]** "the urgency's gonna come from whoever's leading the company." (S09 131:40)
   "So my sense of urgency, I have, like a maniacal sense of urgency." (S09 131:44) Sources: S09, T08, T06, S17.

10. **Iterate fast where failure is cheap; be conservative where it is not. [M6]** "the early ones, you want them to blow up, or you're gonna have to find a place to store them." (S02 22:54) Living with a known problem because it has not failed yet is
    Russian roulette (S02 08:12). Keep the experiment off the working line (S23 08:01). Sources: S02, S01, S23, S22.

11. **Progress is iterations times learning per iteration. [M6, M8]** A design center that cuts a year-long chip cycle to
    "something that would normally take a year, I think we, we, we may be able to reduce to a month." (S23 10:03) is worth
    more than a smarter plan. "You, you do as much as you can on the ground, then you gotta fly it." (S18 40:31) because "So it'll work in sim, but not in reality." (S18 40:31) Sources: S23, S18, S11.

12. **Get one way that works before the best way.** "The first order of business is to get one way at all that works." (S18 56:27)
    On the heat shield: "There's a lot of ways to solve the problem." (S18 20:21) What matters is that one of them
    is solved; which is slightly better comes second (S18 20:43). Sources: S18, S19.

13. **Every dollar out needs a reason and a living owner. [M12]** "So you don't have the, the normal corrective mechanism that you'd have for a company or for an, an individual. The checks just always, they always, always clear." (S20 56:19)
    Comb "through every expense worldwide, no matter how small, and cut everything that doesn't have a strong value justification." (T06) Sources: T06, T12, S20, S21, S22.

14. **Close the loop on reality, keep ego small.** "You want, you want to just close the loop on reality hard." (S06 24:55)
    When your ego-to-ability ratio "gets too high, then you're, you're, you're, you're gonna basically break the feedback loop to reality." (S06 23:59)
    Ask for the bad news on purpose (S11 21:00) and read the data before you conclude (S19 21:11). Sources: S06, S04, S11, S19.

15. **Be useful; money follows.** "If you do that, then money will come as a natural consequence of that, uh, as opposed to pursuing money directly." (S07 112:14) Sources: S07, S06, S03, S13.

16. **A small chance beats no chance, and you say so up front.** He started SpaceX expecting to fail and told recruits:
    "I said we're probably gonna die. Uh, but, uh, small chance we might not die." (S06 18:16) He uses the same honest pitch for
    Mars in 2025 (S22 18:01). Sources: S06, S11, S22, T05.

17. **Put your own money in first, and play the hand yourself.** He put almost all of his Zip2 payout into the
    next company (S06 13:05) and started Tesla with only his own funds because the odds were so low (T05). When he had to
    commit everything to Tesla in 2008, he took the CEO job too: "if you've got all your chips on, on, on the table, you gotta play the hand yourself." (S11 15:41)
    Sources: S06, S11, T05, T01.

18. **Hire for traits you cannot teach; add domain knowledge later. [M11]** "Uh, if so, you can add domain knowledge. Uh, but those, those fundamental traits, those fundamental properties you cannot change." (S09 113:01)
    Leaders should work their way up, not "parachute into being the boss instead of earning it." (S16 42:04) Sources: S09, S16, T06.

19. **Capacity comes from doers, not coordinators. [M11]** Purchase orders do not dig holes (S23 21:02). "So I aspire to be Jose. Um, as SpaceX wants to be a-- is Jose. We do actual work." (S23 21:02) Sources: S23, S17.

20. **Leaders are seen at the work, and do some of it.** "The more senior you are, the more visible must be your presence." (T09)
    He slept under his desk on the factory floor "so during shift change, the entire team could see me." (S17 16:23) In
    2026 he still does manual work on site, to "do at least a little bit of everything so that I'm not, uh, numb to the pain of the people who are actually doing it" (S23 15:00)
    Sources: T09, T08, S06, S17, S23.

21. **Communication takes the shortest path, in plain words.** No chain of command for information (T06), no made-up
    acronyms: "The key test for an acronym is to ask whether it helps or hurts communication." (T02) Org chart errors show
    up in the product (S16 17:27). Sources: T06, T02, S01, S16.

22. **Everyone is chief engineer.** People must understand the whole system well enough to spot a bad local
    optimization: "you really want, uh, everyone to be chief engineer." (S01 38:26) Fifty right answers to fifty local questions can
    be wrong together (S16 18:19). Sources: S01, S16.

23. **Build it yourself when no supplier will, or when the supplier caps your quality.** Early suppliers would not take
    a car startup seriously, so Tesla built parts itself, which "ended up being, um, an asset because now we understood so much about the entire supply chain" (S17 52:17). Outside the core, he leaves things
    to others (S21 31:28). Sources: S17, S16, S20, S23, S21.

24. **Digital work goes to software first, once the step survives the algorithm. [M10]** "But anything that is digital, uh, which is like just someone at a computer doing something, AI is gonna take over those jobs like lightning." (S21 154:26)
    Keep a human check only while the machine is at or below human level (S14 23:41). Sources: S08, S09, S14, S21.
