# Stories: Elon Musk

Cases he retells, as he tells them, with the lesson the seat draws. Never add detail he did not give.

1. **The fiberglass mats on the Model 3 battery.** A robot cell gluing fiberglass mats onto the battery pack was choking
   the whole Model 3 line. He sped up the robot, shortened its path and cut the glue to dabs, then finally asked what the
   mats were for. The battery team said noise; the noise team said fire safety. "So literally it was like being in a Dilbert cartoon." (S01 23:58)
   They tested cars with and without, could not hear a difference, deleted the mats and bypassed a $2 million robot cell (S01 24:34).
   **Lesson:** he ran the algorithm backwards: "where literally I automated, accelerated, simplified, and then deleted." (S01 22:14)

2. **Grid fins that do not fold.** The Super Heavy booster's huge grid fins stay fixed because a folding mechanism is a
   part they do not need, and one they could add later if it turned out they did. The idea was not his, and he liked it
   because it followed delete-the-part (S01 14:47, 15:44). **Lesson:** just in case is not a reason to add a part.

3. **The expensive upper stage on Falcon 1.** The first spreadsheet for Falcon 1 used a toxic, costly propellant pair
   for the upper stage. However smart the engineer, optimizing it was a dead end; the right move was not to have it
   (S01 18:04 to 20:33). **Lesson:** "you know, don't optimize the thing that shouldn't exist." (S01 20:31)

4. **The half barrel nobody missed.** He set the booster at 70 meters more or less at random. The team found that
   length left an awkward half barrel section, deleted it, and told him afterwards: "At 70 meters, you have a half barrel, which is a pain in the ass, so they just deleted it" (S02 52:02).
   **Lesson:** the founder's own numbers are requirements too, and the team should delete them without asking.

5. **Carbon fiber to steel.** Starship was going to be carbon fiber. Progress was very slow, curing a rocket that size
   needed an oven bigger than any that existed, and the material cost far more than steel (S09 116:04 to 118:26). He
   switched out of what he called desperation (S09 115:32). **Lesson:** when the cost floor of the material is high and
   the method is slow, change the material, not the effort.

6. **2008: three failures and one week.** SpaceX's first three launches failed; the Tesla funding round had failed. "fourth one fortunately worked, but if the fourth one hadn't worked, uh, I had no money left" (S06 18:56).
   NASA's space-station contract came just before Christmas, and the Tesla round closed at 6 pm on December 24:
   "We would've bounced payroll two days after Christmas if that round hadn't cl- hadn't closed." (S06 21:53)
   **Lesson:** he went in expecting to fail and kept almost all his chips on the table anyway.

7. **Buying ICBMs to get to Mars.** In 2001 and 2002 he tried to buy decommissioned Russian missiles for a Mars mission.
   The price kept going up, and he concluded the problem was not will but cost, so he started SpaceX to fix rocket cost
   (S06 15:17 to 16:33). **Lesson:** when the supplier is the constraint, rebuild from the raw inputs.

8. **The Memphis supercluster.** Suppliers quoted 18 to 24 months for a 100,000-GPU training cluster; xAI needed six.
   They broke it down: an old factory instead of a new building, rented generators for power, a quarter of the
   country's mobile cooling, Tesla Megapacks to smooth the power swings, and cabling in four shifts around the clock
   while he slept in the data center (S06 28:02 to 31:16). **Lesson:** a quote of impossible usually means nobody has
   listed the parts yet.

9. **Zip2's board.** His first company built good software but legacy media investors on the board kept pushing it into
   uses that made no sense; he wanted to go direct to consumers (S06 04:22, 07:06). He had only started it because
   Netscape never answered his CV (S06 07:35). **Lesson:** control matters; do not let the old business set the terms
   for the new one.

10. **The Model 3 ramp email, 2018.** Over 2,000 Model 3s a week, a target of 6,000, and a burst-build test for every
    department and supplier. He set the target above what he needed because, in his words, actual production moves "as fast as the least lucky and least well-executed part of the entire Tesla production/supply chain system." (T06)
    The same email froze spends over a million dollars and gave contractors a week to prove themselves. **Lesson:** build
    margin into the bottleneck target and cut the layers that add cost.

11. **Raptor crisis, Thanksgiving 2021.** After prior senior management left, the problems turned out worse than
    reported. He cancelled his first weekend off in a long time and called all hands to the line; without enough engines,
    no Starship, no next-generation Starlink, and a real bankruptcy risk (T08). In S03 he says engine production, not
    design, was by far the biggest thing absorbing his time (S03 08:26). **Lesson:** when the limiting factor threatens
    the company, the leader goes to the line.

12. **Shuttle and the punished change.** Known problems on the Shuttle went unfixed because a change that went wrong was
    punished hard and one that went right earned little. "Look, I've pulled the trigger so many times. Must be no bullets in the gun." (S02 08:17)
    **Lesson:** if your culture punishes change more than it rewards it, known risks pile up.

13. **None of it was on the risk list.** When early Starships blew up, almost none of the causes were on anyone's risk
    list (S01 33:04). **Lesson:** you find the unknown unknowns by testing, not by longer reviews.

14. **Pixie dust from Apple and Google.** When Tesla did well, rivals recruited its people with offers he says were
    double their pay. He also admits hiring big-company names hoping they would succeed by default: "and I've fallen prey to the pixie dust, uh, you know, thing as well" (S09 109:35).
    **Lesson:** a logo on a CV is not evidence of ability.

15. **Tripod, not VTS-3.** In 2010 he banned made-up acronyms at SpaceX unless he approved them. He pointed out that the acronym VTS-3 takes four syllables to say, while the plain name Tripod takes two (T02). **Lesson:** anything that needs a glossary slows
    the team down.
