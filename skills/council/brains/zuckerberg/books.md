# Books: Mark Zuckerberg

## His own
He has written no books. His long-form writing in this brain:
- **The IPO letter, The Hacker Way** (2012): mission first, then five values (focus on impact, move fast, be bold, be
  open, build social value). Its money line: "we don't build services to make money; we make money to build better services" (T01).
- **Building Global Community** (2017, T02): the long manifesto on groups, safety and civic life.
- **Year of Efficiency update** (2023, T05): his clearest written operating manual (flatter is faster, leaner is better,
  keep technology the main thing, invest in tools, in-person time).
- **Open Source AI Is the Path Forward** (2024, T08) and **Personal Superintelligence** (2025, T14): why AI should be
  open and in individuals' hands rather than centrally automating work.
- **The Meta Superintelligence Labs memo** (2025, T12): the reboot of Meta's AI effort around a small, dense team.

## People and works he names as influences
- **Peter Thiel and Sean Parker:** "a lot of the, um, the early lessons that I took on how to think about strategy came from Peter and Sean" (S01 27:02). From Thiel: network effects, and picking the one or two things that matter out of a hundred (S01); the line about the biggest risk being no risk (S02). He took Thiel's money because "I felt like he could relate to us on a founder level" (S13 19:20), and says Thiel advised as a founder, not a money manager (S13).
- **Don Graham (The Washington Post):** he nearly took Graham's money over any VC's because Graham's family had run a company for generations; Graham then sent him to Accel and joined the board (S13).
- **Microsoft and Windows:** he studied Microsoft growing up and took from it the idea of building a platform and an ecosystem (S01, S14). At 21 he used it as the case for iterating: "I don't know when the last time was that they had a good product before version four" (S15 31:59).
- **Jeff Bezos:** "Jeff Bezos clearly, I mean, one of the, I think, great entrepreneurs of, of our time" (S13 25:50), as proof that some founders do decide to start a company first; he also cites a talk with Bezos about Seattle job tenure being about twice the Valley's (S13).
- **Steve Jobs:** the computer as a bicycle for the mind, which he uses to define technology as extending a human capacity (S14).
- **Dunbar's number:** about 150 relationships, which he says matched the average friend count at signup and which Facebook extends (S14).
- **Ben Horowitz:** he credits him with the rule to keep the main thing the main thing, as the answer to what limits a big company: management focus, not capital (S05).
- **Satya Nadella:** he told him "you've always been, um, from my perspective, a, a kind of friend and ally on the open source work that we've done" (S18 00:40), and repeats his point that cheaper technology gets consumed more (S18).
- **Alexandr Wang:** the plan Wang sent him in 2025, starting with talent density, shaped the new lab; he calls him the sharpest entrepreneur of his generation he had met (S19).
- **Augustus:** an interest he returns to; he admires how Augustus pushed a positive-sum idea of peace that people of the time could not conceive, and compares it to investors not grasping open source (S05). In college he built a crowd-sourced study tool for an art class on Augustus (S01, S14).
- **The classics:** he planned to major in classics, loved Latin and Greek in high school, and his sister went on to a classics PhD (S14); he still mentions people learning Latin and Greek (S17).
- **Aeschylus:** he wears a shirt with *pathei mathos*, learning through suffering, which he calls a family saying (S06).
- **Jensen Huang:** agrees with Jensen that many founders would not start if they knew how painful it would be (S06).
- **Elon Musk at Twitter:** credits the push for a leaner, more technical org with fewer management layers as good for the industry, without judging the tactics (S04).
- **Books on polarization:** cites Matthew Gentzkow's research and Ezra Klein's *Why We're Polarized* when arguing social media is not the main cause of polarization (S03).
- **Orwell's 1984:** mentioned in passing about content-removal pressure (S07).
- **Culture eats strategy for breakfast:** he cites the saying when explaining that people and culture matter more than the org chart (S11).

Not in this corpus: his 2015 Year of Books reading list.
