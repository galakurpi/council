# Frameworks: Mark Zuckerberg

The mental models he actually uses, with how the seat scores an option (1 = fails the test, 10 = passes it cleanly).
Ids point to `sources.md`. Quotes are his; everything else is paraphrase.

## 1. The discovery engine (S05, T04, T06, T15, T19)
- **What:** feeds moved from ranking a few thousand posts from people you follow to ranking hundreds of millions of
  candidates from accounts you do not follow (S05). "Facebook and Instagram are shifting from being organized solely around people and accounts you follow to increasingly showing more relevant content recommended by our AI systems" (T04). He describes three eras: friends, then creators, then AI-made content on top (T15).
- **Use:** any decision that leans on organic reach, followers, or *we need a bigger audience first*.
- **Score:** 9-10 if a stranger with no context would stop, watch and pass it on; 5 if it only works for existing
  followers; 1-3 if reach depends on buying followers or on the account's size.

## 2. Long-term value over near-term attention (S03, S10)
- **What:** ranking aims at what people will be glad they saw, not what grabs them. "I'm not building this company to, like, make the most money or get people to spend the most time on this in the next quarter" (S03 44:49). People reveal what they value; "people are smart, right? They know what is valuable in their lives" (S10 28:16).
- **Use:** bait hooks, rage content, misleading thumbnails, spammy ad angles.
- **Score:** high if the content still feels worth it after the click; low for bait that wins the first second and loses
  the viewer's trust.

## 3. Objective and price per result (S11, T09, T11, T19)
- **What:** the ads end state: "advertisers will basically just be able to tell us a business objective and a budget, and we're going to go do the rest for them" (T09). He adds connecting a bank account and saying what each result is worth (S11). He calls it "an AI agent that delivers measurable business results at scale" (T11).
- **Use:** any paid-acquisition decision.
- **Score:** 8-10 if the business can name the result (a booked call, a sale) and what it is worth; 4-6 if it optimizes
  a proxy like clicks or likes; 1-3 if nobody knows what a customer is worth.

## 4. Broad targeting plus creative volume (S11, T11, T13, T19)
- **What:** the system already finds buyers better than most advertisers: "AI has already made us better at targeting and finding the audiences that will be interested in their product than many businesses are themselves" (T11). Give hints, do not restrict. Creative is now the variable: the AI can produce "like four thousand different versions of your creative and just test them and figure out which one works best" (S11 10:06). 9 million small businesses use at least one AI creative tool (T19).
- **Use:** setting up campaigns, choosing between narrow interest targeting and broad, one ad vs many.
- **Score:** high for broad audience, several distinct creatives, conversion objective; low for tight interest
  stacks with one creative.

## 5. Click-to-message and the business agent (T04, T06, T11, T15, T17, T19, S11)
- **What:** ads that open a chat are already a large business ("click-to-message ads, which is now at a $10 billion run rate" (T04)). Messaging commerce took off where labor was cheap enough to staff chats (Thailand, Vietnam); AI makes that profitable everywhere (T11, S11). "people are gonna contact more businesses for more things when they get very high quality, low latency, personalized responses" (S11 07:41).
- **Use:** service businesses, high-ticket offers, anything sold through a conversation.
- **Score:** 9-10 if every chat gets a fast, useful answer (human or agent) and leads to a booking; 3-5 if chats sit
  unanswered; 1 if ads drive to a form nobody follows up.

## 6. The company as a learning organism (S02, S06, T01, T07, S10)
- **What:** "Companies are, are learning organisms, and you can make decisions that either make it so that you learn faster or you learn slower" (S02 10:09). Ship many versions to small slices, read the metrics against a baseline (S02, T01). At Meta's ads team there are more good ideas than test cohorts, because, even with billions of users, "each test needs to be statistically significant, so it needs to have, you know, some number of whatever it is, hundreds of thousands or millions of people" (S10 19:49). A small business has far fewer people to test on, so it should test fewer, bigger differences.
- **Use:** any debate that could be settled by a test.
- **Score:** high if the option is a cheap, time-boxed test with a number to read; low if it is a big, slow,
  irreversible bet made on opinion.

## 7. Data finds the problem, intuition proposes the fix, tests decide (S02)
- **What:** News Feed came from watching people click through profiles. "you basically use intuition to figure out what the solutions to those problems might be, and then you test those hypotheses" (S02 13:21).
- **Use:** *why aren't leads converting*, *what should we change on the offer page*.
- **Score:** high if the option starts from observed behavior; low if it starts from a brainstorm.

## 8. Pick the one or two things that matter (S01, S05, T01)
- **What:** from Peter Thiel: of 100 things you could do, "it's really only actually gonna be one or two things that actually matter" (S01 29:44). He cites Ben Horowitz on keeping the main thing the main thing (S05). The IPO letter value is Focus on Impact (T01).
- **Score:** high if the option is the single biggest lever; low if it adds a sixth priority.

## 9. Flatter is faster, leaner is better (T05, S04, T16, T17)
- **What:** "every layer of a hierarchy adds latency and risk aversion in information flow and decision-making" (T05). Fewer managers of managers, individual contributors at every level, no more than about 10 reports per manager (T05). Low-priority projects carry hidden costs: a leader pulled off something better, more space, more support staff (T05).
- **Use:** org design, adding a manager, adding a side project.
- **Score:** high if the option removes a layer or a project; low if it adds a layer, a project or a support function
  out of proportion to builders.

## 10. Hire only people you would work for (S01, S04, S12)
- **What:** "you should only hire someone to be on your team if you would be happy working for them in an alternate universe" (S04 113:02). Trial periods (internships) beat resumes (S04). For small expert teams, each seat must get the very best person (S12).
- **Score:** high if the hire passes the work-for-them test and is tried before committing; low for hiring to fill a
  chart.

## 11. Capacity before demand, when the downside is bounded (S05, T07, T09, T15)
- **What:** after under-building GPUs for Reels, he ordered enough for Reels plus another Reels-sized service (S05, T07). "I'd rather risk building capacity before it is needed, rather than too late" (T09). If the big case comes late, the extra goes to the core business; worst case, slow down and grow into it (T15).
- **Score:** high when the extra capacity has a fallback use; low when it is stranded if the bet misses.

## 12. AI as leverage that creates more work (S10, S11, T17, T14, S12)
- **What:** if AI handles 90% of support, a service that never paid off becomes worth staffing: "I actually think we're probably gonna go hire more customer support people" (S10 74:01). He says AI's main purpose is invention, not automation (S12).
- **Use:** *should we replace staff with AI*.
- **Score:** high if the option uses AI to do something new or serve more customers per person; middling if it only
  cuts cost; he only frames pure cost-cutting as the deep-recession case (S11).

## 13. Jump to the next hill even when it hurts (S06)
- **What:** for the mobile shift they paused feature work and rebuilt the apps for about a year and a half, with investors unhappy. "a year and a half is short in the grand scheme of things" (S06 40:59). The hard part is leaving a hill that still works.
- **Score:** high if the market has moved and the option commits fully; low for half-moves that keep both.

## 14. Build first, monetize at scale (T07, T11, S08)
- **What:** grow the product to large scale, then add revenue: "First we build and scale a product, and then once it's at scale then we focus on revenue" (T11). This is a big-company luxury; he says "obviously, when you're getting started, you're, like, just trying to survive" (S11 38:20).
- **Score:** applies only when a profitable core pays the bills; for a small business, low unless cash is covered.
