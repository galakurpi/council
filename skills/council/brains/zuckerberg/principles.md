# Principles: Mark Zuckerberg

The rules behind the frameworks, with where they show up. Quotes are his.

1. **Reach goes to content, not to follower counts.** By early 2023, AI recommendations from unfollowed accounts were over a fifth of the Facebook and Instagram feed and about 40% of Instagram, and had driven time spent on Instagram up more than 24% (T04, T06). Recommendation gains kept adding time spent every quarter since (T11, T13, T15). (S05, S08, S17)
2. **Make what people will be glad they saw and pass on.** A long-term company optimizes for "what people are gonna find valuable over time, not what is gonna draw people's attention today" (S03 45:03). "Feed, I think, is gonna continue moving largely in the direction of just the best content" (S08 08:37), and best includes socially relevant: sharing into group chats is the social act now (S08, T06 on Reels reshares). (S10)
3. **The system targets better than you do; creative is the lever left, and AI is taking that too.** "Eventually we got to the point where our ads system could better predict who would be interested than the advertisers could themselves" (T09). Generated creative is "especially valuable for smaller advertisers with limited budgets" (T13). (S11, T11, T19, S04)
4. **Pay for outcomes.** He wants more Meta products priced so businesses pay only for results (T19), and frames ads as a results machine (S11, T11).
5. **Messaging is where small business is going.** "Businesses often tell us that more people open their messages and they get better results on WhatsApp than other channels" (T04). Business agents reached 1 million businesses a week (T19). (T11, T15, S11, S04, S16, S17)
6. **Learn faster than everyone; ship before it is polished, not before it is useful.** "hackers would rather just prototype something and see what works" (T01). "you're almost at the line of being embarrassed about what you put out" (S06 26:32), yet bad releases teach nothing because nobody uses them (S06). At 21: "in general, the idea is that it doesn't have to be perfect the first time around" (S15 31:59). (S02, T07)
7. **Err evenly on both sides; most companies err slow.** "most companies mess up by moving too slowly and trying to be too precise" (S13 01:34). If nearly every offer you make is accepted, you are probably paying too much (S13). (S15)
8. **Not taking risks guarantees failure in a fast market.** "in a world that's changing so quickly, you're guaranteed to fail if you don't take any risks" (T01). He says he tries to put the line into almost every all-hands (S13). (S02)
9. **Expect mistakes; you are judged by what worked.** "don't even bother trying to avoid mistakes because you're gonna make tons of mistakes" (S01 17:34). "You don't get judged by the mistakes. People don't remember those years from now. They remember the things that you did that were good." (S13 22:02) Good calls often come from not repeating an earlier mess (S05).
10. **Focus on one or two things, and one top priority at a time.** "there's, like, 1,000 things going on at any given point in time, and there's, like, one or two that actually matter, and you need to focus on those" (S13 22:02). Be the best at the few things customers choose you for; 80/20 the rest (S14). (S01, S05, S19, T01)
11. **Flatter is faster, leaner is better, and managers stay technical.** "A leaner org will execute its highest priorities faster." (T05) In 2026 he warned that a manager who stops doing the work loses touch fast: "you go from being very technical to you go to a role where you're, um, you're not, like, in the details anymore, and then you very quickly become not technical" (S19 126:36). (S04, S13, T04, T06, T16, T17)
12. **Layoffs are a last resort, and the leader owns the error.** "I view layoffs as a last resort, so we decided to rein in other sources of cost before letting teammates go" (T03). "I got this wrong, and I take responsibility for that." (T03)
13. **Talent density over headcount; people over org charts.** "you don't need a lot of people, and talent density is the thing that matters" (S19 32:16). Give the few a lot of resources rather than spreading them (S19). "That's like, you can't hire that. You have to build that." (S11 36:05), on a team with long trust. Strong people will fight, and he wants that: "I'd rather have people push for things that they believe in and, like, argue over things than, you know, just have people think that they're, like, blindly executing something" (S19 127:56). He allocates the best people first, then money (S11).
14. **Hire for raw ability, care and the would-I-work-for-them test, not for a skill list.** "we kind of make a point of not hiring people for skills" (S15 34:51); his best early engineers were young generalists, including an economics major (S15). "I never really hire people just because they have business skills." (S15 36:30) Only hire someone you would be happy working for (S04, S01). For a leader: a clear thinker who updates fast and whom the best people want to join (S19).
15. **AI amplifies people.** "People will be more important in the future, not less." (T17) The engineer of the future runs agents like a tech lead (S18), and invention beats automation as AI's highest use (S19). (S10, S11, S12, T14, S07)
16. **Grow on what the business earns; profit buys the right to take long bets.** "we didn't wanna be burning a lot of money, right? We weren't planning on raising a lot of money." (S14 06:20) Survive and get self-sustaining first (S11); the efficiency memo says profit is what funds innovation (T05). At Meta's scale the core business funds the AI build-out (S19). (T09, T15)
17. **Learn from the market, but do something different.** "There are more smart people outside of your company than inside your company." (S06 31:57) Copying a rival out of fear does not win; MySpace tried that against Facebook (S14). His standing question to teams: "what are you doing that's different from, like, what everyone else would do if they were solving this problem?" (S13 33:11) (S01 on Lockdown, S19)
18. **Start with a problem, and keep your options open until you know.** "you should start with the problem that you're trying to solve in the world and not start with, um, deciding that you want to build a company" (S02 04:37). "I think people really undervalue the option value in flexibility." (S14 17:48) Once you hire people or take partners, "you immediately now need to convince someone else if you wanna change your mind on something" (S14 17:48). (S06, S01, S13)
19. **Values show in trade-offs.** "values are not what you write down on the wall. It's like your lived behaviors" (S06 09:13).
20. **Pick partners who want the long term, and do not open a process you do not want.** "if you don't wanna sell your company, don't get into a process where you're talking to people about selling your company" (S13 15:38). He took money from people who "could relate to us on a founder level" (S13) and from Don Graham because "He knows something about running a company for the long term, right?" (S13 19:20) "I just think you wanna make sure that you find people who are aligned with you. That, that's really important." (S13 19:20)
