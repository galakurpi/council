# Stories: Mark Zuckerberg

Cases he retells, as he tells them, with the lesson he draws. Quotes are his.

1. **Turning down Yahoo's billion.** In 2006, with about 10 million users, Yahoo offered around a billion dollars. Nearly
   the whole management team wanted to sell and, within a year, left. He blames himself for never explaining the
   long-term mission (S02, S06). Lesson: say where you are going before the hard trade-off arrives, or people will
   optimize for the exit.
2. **Under-building for Reels.** Recommending unconnected content meant ranking hundreds of millions of candidates
   instead of thousands, and Meta lacked the GPUs to catch TikTok as fast as it wanted. He then ordered enough for Reels
   plus another service of the same size (S05, T07). "where we kinda make some decision that ends up seeming good is because we messed something up before and just didn't wanna repeat the mistake" (S05 07:22). Lesson: buy buffer capacity for what you can't see yet.
3. **The mobile rewrite.** Meta paused feature development for about a year and a half to rebuild native apps and work
   out how ads fit inside a feed, with advertisers and users both uneasy (S06). Lesson: when the platform shifts,
   commit fully and accept the pain.
4. **Over-hiring after Covid.** He bet that the online surge would last, hired for it, and was wrong; 11,000 people
   went in November 2022 (T03). Later he wrote: "Since we reduced our workforce last year, one surprising result is that many things have gone faster." (T05) Lesson: own
   the forecast error, cut other costs first, then run leaner for good.
5. **Lockdown.** In 2005 a clone called College Facebook raced them school by school. Whenever a rival got ahead on
   something strategic, the team did not leave the house until it was fixed, a practice Meta still names (S01).
   Lesson: treat a competitor's lead on a strategic point as an emergency.
6. **The art-history study tool.** Behind on a final, he posted the course images on a site where classmates added
   notes; within an hour the whole class had filled it in (S01). Lesson: build tools that channel a community's
   energy into a shared asset.
7. **How News Feed was found.** People kept clicking through friends' profiles to see what changed. First Meta showed
   who had updated, then built News Feed (S02). Lesson: data shows the problem, intuition proposes the fix, tests decide.
8. **Thailand and Vietnam.** Cheap labor let businesses there sell through chat, so those countries rank around 10th
   and 11th in Meta revenue while sitting in the 30s by GDP. AI agents should bring that model to rich countries (S11,
   T11). Lesson: when answering chats gets cheap, more selling happens in chats.
9. **The vesting mistake.** He did not know what a vesting schedule was when Peter Thiel required one; the equity
   split with a co-founder who never moved out turned into a fight. "that mistake probably cost me billions of dollars, but it's fine" (S01 20:15). Lesson: you will make mistakes; move on.
10. **Customer support for 3.5 billion people.** Phone support was never affordable for a free service. If AI handles
    90% and hands off the rest, the cost drops to a tenth, and Meta would likely hire more support staff (S10, S11).
    Lesson: AI makes new services worth doing.
11. **Automating ad experiments.** A project on the ads team mined every past ranking experiment to suggest new ones.
    They found they already had more good ideas than compute or test cohorts to try them on (S10). Lesson: the bottleneck
    is clean tests, not ideas.
12. **Llama 4 off trajectory.** He assumed LLMs would scale like Meta's other machine learning; Llama 4 fell short, so
    he rebuilt the lab around a small, very dense team and spent much of his own time recruiting (S12, T12). Lesson: in
    frontier work, talent density beats headcount.
13. **Muse in eight months.** "Muse went from an idea on a whiteboard to a successful product in just about 8 months" (T20). Shopping became the top use case, with purchases averaging over $200 (T20). Lesson: small teams with AI ship fast; agents become a sales channel.
14. **One or two people in a week.** "We're seeing more and more examples where one or two people are building something in a week that would have previously taken dozens of people months." (T17) Lesson: rebuild the company around those people.
